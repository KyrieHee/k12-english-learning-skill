# Curriculum Framework

Use this reference to align plans with China K12 English stages. The stable backbone is:

- National curriculum expectations first.
- Local textbook and exam requirements second.
- Tutoring-institution patterns only as workflow inspiration.

## Core Principles

For compulsory education, organize English learning around language ability, cultural awareness, thinking quality, and learning ability. For senior high school, continue these core competencies with stronger discourse, academic reading, writing, and exam-oriented application.

When building plans, translate broad curriculum language into concrete learner actions:

- Language ability: listen, speak, read, view, write, vocabulary, grammar, discourse.
- Cultural awareness: topic background, cross-cultural comparison, respectful expression.
- Thinking quality: infer, compare, summarize, evaluate, justify with evidence.
- Learning ability: preview, note-taking, retrieval, self-correction, reflection.

## Stage Bands

### Primary Lower

Typical focus:

- Phonics and sound-letter awareness.
- Classroom language and daily routines.
- High-frequency nouns, verbs, adjectives, numbers, colors, family, school, food, animals.
- Simple patterns: greetings, self-introduction, likes, possession, simple questions.
- Listening before reading; oral response before written output.

Avoid:

- Heavy grammar terminology.
- Long copying tasks.
- Abstract test-analysis language.

Best outputs:

- 5-minute preview card.
- Listen-and-point task.
- Picture-supported sentence substitution.
- Parent-child oral check.

### Primary Upper

Typical focus:

- Topic vocabulary across school life, seasons, hobbies, travel, community, health.
- Sentence patterns and short paragraphs.
- Basic grammar awareness: tense, plural, pronouns, prepositions, question forms.
- Reading short texts for main idea and details.
- Guided writing: sentence chain, short message, mini paragraph.

Best outputs:

- Unit topic map.
- Vocabulary grouping and collocation list.
- Sentence-pattern drill.
- Short reading with 3 evidence questions.
- 60-100 word guided writing, adjusted by grade.

### Junior Middle

Typical focus:

- Systematic grammar: tense, voice, clauses, non-finite forms at age-appropriate level, articles, prepositions, pronouns, adjectives/adverbs.
- Exam question types: listening, single choice where relevant, cloze, reading comprehension, task-based reading, grammar filling, sentence transformation, writing.
- Reading accuracy and speed.
- Writing structure, tense consistency, topic coverage, sentence variety.

Best outputs:

- Unit knowledge map.
- Grammar rule plus contrast examples.
- Question-type drill.
- Mistake classification.
- Timed mini test.

### Senior High

Typical focus:

- Discourse-level reading and evidence.
- Long sentence parsing.
- Advanced vocabulary and collocations.
- Writing: application writing, continuation writing where relevant, argumentation, summary/response depending on local exam.
- 高考-style listening, reading, 七选五, cloze, grammar filling, writing.

Best outputs:

- Text genre and discourse structure map.
- Long-sentence analysis.
- Vocabulary in context.
- Exam-style timed practice.
- Writing rubric and revision.

## Learning Goal Types

### School Sync

Use the textbook unit order. Keep output close to classroom content:

- Preview before class.
- Key points after class.
- Same-week consolidation.
- Unit test rehearsal.

### Catch-Up

Find the earliest missing prerequisite. Avoid jumping into current unit only:

- Diagnose foundational gaps.
- Repair one gap at a time.
- Reconnect to current textbook.
- Keep success criteria small and visible.

### Score Improvement

Start from score loss:

- Which question types lose points?
- Which errors repeat?
- Which knowledge points are unstable?
- Which task timing is weak?

Build cycles around weak question types, not around vague "learn more English".

### Enrichment

Only expand after school basics are stable:

- Graded reading.
- Oral presentation.
- Writing polish.
- Cross-cultural topics.
- Project-based output.

## Difficulty Control

Adjust by:

- Text length.
- Vocabulary density.
- Grammar terminology.
- Number of tasks.
- Time limit.
- Whether output is oral, written, or test-style.

When uncertain, default to one level easier for explanation and one level target-appropriate for practice.

## Policy And Workload

Keep learning efficient and age-appropriate. Prefer retrieval, explanation, correction, and reuse over repetitive mechanical workload. Do not promise guaranteed score increases or portray heavy off-campus tutoring as necessary.
